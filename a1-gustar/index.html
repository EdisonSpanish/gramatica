Gustar

Verbs like gustar

Explanation

  • me · te · le · nos · lesthe person who likes
  • gustaone thing or an infinitive
  • gustanseveral things

Gustar does not work like English like. In Me gusta el té, the tea is the subject, and me is the person who feels the pleasure. That is why the verb agrees with the thing, not with “I”: Me gusta el té (one thing), Me gustan los tés (several things). With an infinitive, the verb stays singular: Me gusta caminar.

The person who likes something is me, te, le, nos, les. For usted, él and ella the form is le; you can make the person clear with A Marta le gusta… or A usted le gusta….

Two very common slips come from English. First, conjugating gustar as if you were the subject: Me gusto el cine (that would not mean “I like the movies”). Second, dropping the article: Me gusta café. Name the thing with el, la, los or las, or use an infinitive. To ask, use ¿Te gusta…? or, with usted, ¿Le gusta…?

How gustar works

  • (a mí)me
  • (a ti)te
  • (a él / a ella / a usted)le
  • (a nosotros / a nosotras)nos
  • (a ellos / a ellas / a ustedes)les
+
gusta + one thing or an infinitive

el café · la música · caminar

gustan + several things

los museos · las películas

The verb agrees with the thing you like, not with the person. The person is the pronoun. Example: Me gusta el café. Me gustan los museos.

Ejemplos · Examples

  • ☕

    Me gusta el café por la mañana.

    I like coffee in the morning.

  • 🎬

    Me gustan las películas de humor.

    I like comedy movies.

  • 🚶

    Te gusta caminar por el río, ¿verdad?

    You like walking by the river, don't you?

  • 🤫

    A Pablo le gusta el silencio en la oficina.

    Pablo likes the quiet in the office.

  • 🍳

    Nos gusta cocinar el domingo.

    We like cooking on Sundays.

  • 💺

    ¿Le gusta a usted este asiento? —Sí, me gusta.

    Do you like this seat? —Yes, I do.
